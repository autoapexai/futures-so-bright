The short version

  • Future's So Bright is a free, G-rated game for kids and families.
  • No ads. No selling or renting your information. No chat, no accounts and no way for players to message each other.
  • You can play the whole game without telling us who you are.
  • The game counts visits anonymously. You can turn that off (see Your choices).
  • The parts that ask for a name, an email or a file are for grown-ups, behind an "Ask a grown-up!" check.

Who we are

Future's So Bright (autoapexai.com) is made by Daniel Bilthouse ("Dan"), the game's creator. "We" and "us" on this page mean Dan. Questions? Email dan@danbilthouse.com.

What the game collects

1. Anonymous visit counts

So Dan can see how many people play, the game counts two things: opening the game, and starting a run. Here is exactly what it sends:

  • A scrambled visitor code. Your browser makes a random ID and keeps it in its own storage (localStorage) on your device. Only a SHA-256 hash of that ID is sent. That is a one-way scrambled code: it can't be turned back into the ID, and it isn't your name or anything about you.
  • Your device type: just "phone", "tablet" or "desktop".
  • The name of the website that sent you, if another site linked to the game (only the site's name, like example.com, never the page address).
  • For a run start: the mode and the starting level.

The visit counter does not use cookies and does not store your IP address, your name, your browser's full details or any "fingerprint" of your device. You can switch it off any time (see Your choices).

2. Leaderboard scores (only if you post one)

If you type initials to put a score on the online leaderboard, we store:

  • Your 3-character initials, your score, the run time, the level you reached and the level you started on, and the mode your run ended in (plus how many modes you used).
  • A scrambled code made from your internet (IP) address. The server mixes your IP address with a secret random value and runs it through SHA-256, a one-way scramble. The real IP address is never saved. This code is used only to stop spam and cheating, for example to allow no more than 5 scores every 10 minutes.
  • A scrambled claim code. When your score is saved, your device gets a random "claim token" that stays in your browser. We keep only a SHA-256 scramble of it, so the game can recognize that a score came from your device (for example, to know if you hold the #1 spot). It doesn't say who you are.
  • For a new #1 score, and for runs at level 11 and up, a short alert note for Dan with the initials, score and level, plus a simple browser type like "iPhone / Safari". The browser's full details are not stored. Level 11+ runs also get a one-time run ticket; we keep only a scramble of it, together with the scrambled internet-address code above.

Scores from a hidden bonus mode go to a separate practice board with the same kind of information. Everyone can see the initials, scores and levels on the boards. Nothing else on this list is shown to other players.

Rude initials are masked. Initials can only use the letters, symbols and kid-friendly emoji in the game's picker. Rude combinations are turned away, and any that slip through show up as *** everywhere in the game.

3. The "Desert Search Party Report"

When a run misses the leaderboard, the game shows a silly message, and it may tell other players' games which message it was. That report holds only a message number (0 to 99) from the game's own built-in list. No initials, score, ID, device or typed text. Reports are cleared out after about an hour. Turning off visit counting also turns these reports off.

4. The waitlist page

Sign-ups on the waitlist page are switched off right now, so its email box does not send your email or name anywhere. If someone tries to sign up, the page sends a note that an attempt failed (just the reason, like "no form yet", and the page's ?reason= label). No email, name or ID is sent, and the page may remember on your own device that you tried. If sign-ups are ever switched on, we will update this policy first.

5. TIME: the suggestion form

On the VALUE FOR VALUE card, TIME lets you send an idea, a joke or a bug report. We store:

  • Your message (up to 1,000 characters).
  • An optional name or initials and an optional email, only if you add them so Dan can reply. These boxes stay hidden until a grown-up passes the "Ask a grown-up!" check. Kids can send just the message.
  • The same scrambled visitor code as the visit counter (above), used only to stop spam (3 messages an hour).

Kids: please don't put your name, address, school or other personal details in your message.

6. TALENT: the resume form (grown-ups only)

TALENT lets a grown-up send a resume. It is behind the "Ask a grown-up!" check. We store the name, email, an optional note, the resume file (PDF, DOC or DOCX, up to 5 MB) with its file name, type and size, and the scrambled visitor code (to stop spam). The file goes into private storage that only Dan can open. The public can't see, list or download it.

7. What stays only on your device

Your browser's storage (localStorage) keeps things like your best score, your local leaderboard, your settings (language, left or right hand, difficulty, game speed), whether you finished How to Play, your claim tokens, the random visitor ID and your visit-counting choice. These stay on your device. You can erase them by clearing this site's data in your browser.

8. Reading the boards

The game also loads the top scores and the latest Desert Search Party Report from our database. Those requests don't send anything about you. The game's fonts, pictures and sounds all come from our own site.

Kids and parents

  • We do not collect personal information (like a name, email or file) from kids without a parent's consent. That's why the parts that ask for them are behind an "Ask a grown-up!" check.
  • Kids under 13: please ask a grown-up first, and don't send us your name, email or files.
  • If we learn that a child sent us personal information without a parent's consent, we delete it.
  • Parents and guardians can ask to see or delete their child's information, or ask us to stop using it. Email dan@danbilthouse.com. Because the game doesn't know who anyone is, tell us what to look for (for example, the initials and score with the day and time, the message, or the email used) so we can find it.

Who helps run the game

  • Supabase hosts our database and file storage (visit counts, scores, reports and form messages). Like any web service, Supabase sees your internet address when your device connects and may keep it in its standard logs. See Supabase's privacy policy.
  • GitHub Pages hosts the website. Like most web hosts, GitHub may keep standard server logs (such as IP addresses and the pages requested) under GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Venmo, only if a grown-up chooses to send a donation (see below).

We don't sell, rent or trade anyone's information, and we don't share it for advertising. We'd only share it if the law requires it, or to protect players and the game from harm.

TREASURE: donations on Venmo (grown-ups 18+)

TREASURE is an optional, real-money donation, and it's for grown-ups 18 and older only. Tapping it first shows an "Ask a grown-up!" card with a quick check. After that, a grown-up can choose to go to Venmo, a separate company with its own privacy policy and terms.

The game never sees any card, bank or other payment details. Venmo shows Dan what it shows anyone who gets a Venmo payment (like the sender's Venmo name and the payment note). A donation unlocks nothing in the game: everyone gets the same game. Future's So Bright isn't a charity, so donations aren't tax-deductible.

Value for value: if the game gives you a million dollars' worth of fun, donate a million dollars. A dollar's worth? Donate a dollar. Any amount, totally voluntary.

How long we keep things

  • Desert Search Party Reports: about an hour.
  • Visit counts: kept to see how the game does over time. They are anonymous counts, not profiles.
  • Leaderboard scores and alerts: kept while the boards are running. We may remove any score at any time.
  • TIME messages and TALENT resumes: kept only as long as needed to read and answer them. There is no automatic timer, so just ask and we'll delete yours.
  • Waitlist attempt notes: kept only until they're no longer useful.

Keeping it safe

Everything travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS). The database is locked so the public can't read it directly. The game can only use a few narrow doors, like "save a score" or "show the top scores". Resumes sit in private storage, and the game's database keeps internet addresses only as scrambled codes. No system is perfect, but we work to keep things safe and to collect as little as we can.

Your choices

  • Turn off visit counting: open the game with ?notrack=1 at the end of the address, like autoapexai.com/?notrack=1. Your browser remembers this, so the counter stays off on this browser until you open autoapexai.com/?notrack=0 or clear this site's data. It also turns off Desert Search Party Reports. Or use the button below.
  • Only post a score, or send a form, if you want to. The game works fine without them.
  • Clear this site's data in your browser to erase what's stored on your device.
  • Email us to see or delete information about you or your child.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update this page and the "Effective" date at the top. If a change is big, we'll make it easy to notice in the game or on this site.
